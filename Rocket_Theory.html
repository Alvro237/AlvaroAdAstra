Rocket Theory

Key concepts and equations for rocket design, propulsion, and performance. The basis of solid propulsion — essential knowledge for any rocket propulsion engineer, and always worth a refresh.

01 · Design

Rocket Design and Performance

The Tsiolkovsky Rocket Equation

The ideal velocity gain imparted by a propulsion system is described by the Tsiolkovsky Rocket Equation. It relates the change in velocity to the mass ratio before and after the burn.

$$\Delta V_{id}=g_{e}I_{sp}\ln\left(\frac{m_{o}}{m_{f}}\right)=\Delta V+\Delta V_{g}+\Delta V_{D}+\Delta V_{S}$$

  • $\Delta V_{id}$: Ideal velocity gain.
  • $\Delta V$: Mission velocity requirement.
  • $\Delta V_{g}$: Gravity losses — $\int_{0}^{t} g(t)\, dt$.
  • $\Delta V_{D}$: Drag losses — $\int_{0}^{t} \frac{D}{m}\, dt$.
  • $\Delta V_{S}$: Steering losses.
  • $m_{o}$: Initial mass.   $m_{f}$: Final mass.

First-order estimates often drop the complex terms. Did you know we need 11.2 km/s to escape Earth's gravity?

Structural Efficiency & Mass Ratios

Structural efficiency is measured by the propellant mass fraction $\xi$. We want it as close to 1 as possible.

$$\xi = \frac{m_{p}}{m_{p} + m_{i}}$$

  • $m_{p}$: Propellant mass.   $m_{i}$: Inert (structural) mass.   $m_{pl}$: Payload mass.

The mass ratio for any stage $i$:

$$MR_{i}=\frac{m_{o}}{m_{f}}=\frac{m_{pl}+m_{p}+m_{i}}{m_{pl}+m_{i}}$$

From this, the propellant mass needed for any stage:

$$m_{p_{i}}=m_{pl_{i}}\frac{MR_{i}-1}{MR_{i}-1/\xi_{i}}$$

02 · Propulsion

Rocket Propulsion Equations

Thrust Equation

Derived via a control-volume approach on the Navier-Stokes equations:

$$T=\dot{m}_{e}U_{e}+(P_{e}-P_{a})A_{e}$$

  • $\dot{m}_{e}$: Mass flow rate.   $U_{e}$: Exhaust velocity.
  • $P_{e}$: Exit pressure.   $P_{a}$: Ambient pressure.   $A_{e}$: Exit area.

Maximum efficiency at $P_e = P_a$ (perfectly expanded). In vacuum ($P_a = 0$) thrust is maximised.

Specific Impulse

$I_{sp}$ is the key figure of merit for comparing engine efficiency:

$$I_{sp}=\frac{T}{\dot{m}g_{e}}$$

Higher $I_{sp}$ → more efficient engine. It relates thrust to propellant consumption.

Characteristic Velocity

$c^{*}$ measures propellant performance and depends on propellant composition:

$$c^{*}=\sqrt{\frac{g_{e}RT_{c}}{\gamma}}\left(\frac{2}{\gamma+1}\right)^{\frac{\gamma+1}{2(\gamma-1)}}$$

The mass flow rate is related to $c^{*}$ by: $\dot{m} = \frac{P_{c}A_{t}}{c^{*}}$

03 · Solid Motors

Solid Rocket Motors

Solid motors combine oxidizer and fuel into a single propellant grain using a binder. The burning rate follows Vieille's law:

$$\dot{r} = aP_{c}^{n}$$

At steady-state (choked nozzle, no mass accumulation), chamber pressure is:

$$P_{c}=\left[\frac{a\rho_{p}A_{s}c^{*}}{g_{e}A_{t}}\right]^{\frac{1}{1-n}}$$

04 · Hybrid

Hybrid Rockets

Hybrid rockets pair a solid fuel with a liquid oxidizer. The oxidizer flow rate is controllable, making the regression rate a function of oxidizer mass flux:

$$\dot{r} = aG_{ox}^{n}, \quad G_{ox} = \frac{\dot{m}_{ox}}{A_{p}}$$

where $A_{p}$ is the port area. This controllability gives hybrids a safety and throttling advantage over solid motors.

05 · Liquid

Liquid Rocket Engines

Liquid rockets offer the largest controllability and thrust of any propulsion class — the go-to choice for orbital launchers.

Propellant Feeding Systems

  • Cold Gas Thrusters: Compressed inert gas (e.g. N₂). Lowest thrust; used for attitude control.
  • Pressure-Fed: High-pressure inert gas pushes propellants into the chamber. Simple and reliable but heavy tanks limit use to smaller engines.
  • Pump-Fed: Turbopumps deliver propellant from low-pressure tanks. Lighter, higher performance — standard for high-thrust engines.

Engine Cycles: Open vs. Closed

  • Open Cycle (Gas Generator): A bleed of propellant burns in a separate gas generator to drive turbopumps; exhaust is dumped overboard. Simple but slightly less efficient.
  • Closed Cycle (Staged Combustion): All propellant routes back into the main chamber after driving turbopumps. More complex but more efficient — no propellant is wasted.

Closed-Cycle Variants

  • Fuel-Rich Staged Combustion: Preburner runs fuel-rich to drive turbines; exhaust completes combustion in the main chamber. Very high efficiency and chamber pressure, but risk of carbon deposits.
  • Oxidizer-Rich Staged Combustion: Oxygen-rich preburner drives turbines. Avoids carbon issues but creates an extremely oxidizing environment requiring exotic materials. Higher explosion risk.
  • Full-Flow Staged Combustion (FFSC): Two preburners (fuel-rich + oxidizer-rich) route all propellant through turbines before entering the main chamber. Highest theoretical efficiency, very high chamber pressure, longer turbomachinery life — but the most complex design. Used in SpaceX Raptor.
