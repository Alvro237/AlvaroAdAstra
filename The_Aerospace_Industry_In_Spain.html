The aerospace industry in Spain, on one map

When I started looking at where I could work after my master's, I ran into a surprisingly basic problem: there was no single place to see the Spanish aerospace industry. There are association member lists, regional cluster websites, official registries and business directories, but each one shows only a slice, and none of them tells you where the companies actually are. If you want to know which space companies exist around Madrid, or who does composites in Andalusia, you end up opening twenty tabs.

So I built the tool I was missing. The Industry Map gathers more than 900 aeronautics, space and defence companies in Spain into one interactive map. Every company has a card with its sector, its activity, a short description, its website and all the sites we could locate, and you can filter the whole map in a couple of taps.

This article explains what the map can do, how the filters work, who I had in mind while building it and, finally, where the data comes from. It is a pre-alpha, so expect rough edges. Let us dive into it!

What you can do with it

The map is designed to answer a simple question quickly: who is doing what, and where? These are the main things you can do with it:

  • Explore by zoom: at country level, companies are grouped into numbered circles. Zoom in, or tap a circle, and they split into individual pins. This keeps the map readable even in hubs with hundreds of companies.
  • Read a company card: tap any pin to open its card with the sectors it works in, the type of activity, a short description, its tags (cluster memberships, approvals...), the list of sites and a link to its website. The map moves so the pin stays visible next to the card.
  • See every site: many companies have more than one location (headquarters, plants, offices). Each one appears as its own pin, and from the card you can jump between them.
  • Browse crowded spots: when several companies share the same town, tapping the group fans the pins out and opens a list of every company there, in alphabetical order, so you can go straight to the one you want.
  • Search: type a company name or a city in the search box. Results update as you type, and pressing Enter zooms the map to the matches.
  • Read the colours: pins are coloured by sector: aeronautics, space and defence. A company that works in several sectors gets a pin split between their colours.
  • Use it on any screen: on a phone or a tablet, the filters open as a full panel and the company card slides up from the bottom, so it works one-handed on the train as well as on a desktop.

The filters

The Filters button opens three groups. Inside a group, selecting several options shows companies that match any of them; between groups, the conditions add up. For example, Space + Engineering / R&D + CITT-Espacio shows the space engineering companies listed by that Madrid cluster. Each option shows how many companies you would get, and the counts update live as you combine them.

  • Sector: Aeronautics, Space and Defence. A company can belong to more than one.
  • Activity: MRO / maintenance, Drones / UAS, Engineering / R&D, Structures & composites, Engines & propulsion, Machining & components, Space systems, Testing & inspection and Startups / ESA BIC.
  • Network or cluster: Andalucía Aerospace, Aerópolis (Seville), HEGAN (Basque Country), Espai Aero CV (Valencia), the Galician cluster, AERA (Aragon), CAAC (Canary Islands), Castilla-La Mancha, CITT-Espacio, TEDAE, AESA Part 145 and AESA POA. This tells you the company appears in that cluster or official list; its sites can be in another region.

When you are happy with the selection, Show N companies closes the panel and zooms to the results, and Clear filters takes you back to the full map. Filters and search work together, so you can, for instance, look for every MRO organisation around a given city.

Who it is for

I built this map first and foremost for people looking for a job or an internship in the sector. When you are a student, you usually know the five big names, but the Spanish industry is made of hundreds of smaller companies: suppliers, engineering firms, maintenance shops, test labs and young space startups. Many of them are hiring and very few students have heard of them. With the map you can:

  • Find companies near you, or near the city you want to move to, and check the commute before applying.
  • Target your speciality: if you love propulsion, structures or simulation, filter by that activity and build a list of companies to contact.
  • Discover the hubs: see at a glance how the industry concentrates in areas such as Madrid, Andalusia, the Basque Country or Catalonia, and which smaller regions also have an aerospace community.
  • Prepare an application: the card gives you the activity and the website, a good starting point to learn about a company before an interview.

It is also useful for engineers changing jobs, students looking for a thesis or a company for their TFG/TFM, companies scouting suppliers or partners in a given region, and anyone curious about how big and how varied the Spanish aerospace ecosystem really is. If you have ever wondered "who does this in Spain?", the map is for you.

A pre-alpha: what to keep in mind

The data was compiled by hand from public sources, and an industry this size changes every month. A few things to keep in mind:

  • Locations: a pin may point to the municipality, or to the registered office, rather than to the exact plant or office. Within a town, companies are grouped on the same spot on purpose.
  • Coverage: some companies may be missing, and a few may have moved, merged or closed. Inclusion is not an endorsement.
  • Language: the map interface is in English, while company descriptions are kept in their original Spanish for now.
  • Check before you go: if a company interests you, take two minutes to confirm its address and activity on its website. It is the quickest way to be sure.

If you spot an error or a missing company, I would love to hear about it through the contact section. Every correction makes the map better for the next person.

Sources

The company list was built by merging and de-duplicating the following public sources (consulted in October 2026), and then completing each company's activity and sites from its own website and public business registries:

  • Business directory: eInforma, companies registered under CNAE 3031 and 3032 (civil and military aeronautical and space construction).
  • National association: TEDAE, current members.
  • Spanish Aviation Safety Agency (AESA): Part 145 maintenance organisations and production organisations (POA, 2023 list).
  • Regional clusters: Andalucía Aerospace, Aerópolis, HEGAN, Espai Aero CV, Consorcio Aeroespacial Gallego, AERA, CAAC, Castilla-La Mancha aeronautics cluster and CITT-Espacio.
  • Startups: ESA BIC incubatees and alumni registry.
  • Reference catalogues: Plataforma Aeroespacial Española, Spanish aerospace ecosystem and the Ministry of Defence catalogue of the Spanish defence industry, aerospace sector.
  • Map: base map © OpenStreetMap contributors, built with Leaflet and Leaflet.markercluster.

I hope this map saves you some of the twenty tabs I had to open, and helps you find a place in this industry where you can grow. The sector in Spain is much bigger and more diverse than it looks from the outside, and there is room for all kinds of engineers. Have a look, play with the filters, and good luck with the search!
